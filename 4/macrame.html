Macramé

What it is

Macramé is the art of making textiles by tying knots in cord or rope, with no loom, needle or weaving involved. Makers use a small set of basic knots, such as the square knot, the half hitch and the lark's head knot, and repeat or combine them to build patterns. Materials like cotton cord, jute, hemp and nylon are common. Macramé is used to make wall hangings, plant hangers, bags, belts and jewelry.

A brief history

Knotting cord into decorative patterns is an old craft, and the word macramé is often traced to an Arabic word for a fringe or veil. Knotted fringes were used to finish woven cloth in the Middle East, and the technique spread to Europe through trade and travel. Sailors also tied knotted items during long voyages. Macramé became fashionable in Victorian England as a home craft, and it had a major revival in the 1970s, when plant hangers and wall hangings became popular. Today it is enjoying a new wave of interest as both a craft and a form of fiber art.

Quick facts

  • Material: cord or rope such as cotton, jute or hemp
  • Technique: knots only, with no loom or needle
  • Built from a few basic knots, repeated in patterns
  • Used for wall hangings, plant hangers and bags
