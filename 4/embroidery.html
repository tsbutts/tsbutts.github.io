Embroidery

What it is

Embroidery is the art of decorating fabric by stitching designs onto it with a needle and thread. Unlike weaving, which builds the cloth itself, embroidery adds patterns, pictures or lettering to cloth that already exists. Embroiderers use threads of cotton, wool, silk or metal, and they can combine many kinds of stitches to create texture, outline shapes and fill in color. It is used on clothing, banners, household linens and wall hangings.

A brief history

Embroidery has been practiced for thousands of years in cultures around the world. In China, silk embroidery was a highly prized art, and in medieval England, skilled workshops produced richly stitched religious garments. The famous Bayeux Tapestry, made in the 1000s, is actually an embroidery, since its scenes are stitched onto linen cloth. Many regions developed their own styles, such as Kantha stitching in South Asia and Otomi embroidery in Mexico. Embroidery is still popular today as both a folk craft and a fine art.

Quick facts

  • Material: fabric, plus thread of cotton, wool, silk or metal
  • Tools: a needle, thread and often an embroidery hoop
  • Additive: designs are stitched onto finished cloth
  • Used on clothing, banners, linens and wall hangings
