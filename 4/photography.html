Photography

What it is

Photography is the art of making images by capturing light. A camera focuses light through a lens onto a light-sensitive surface, which was once chemically treated film or plates and is now an electronic sensor. Photographers choose what to frame, when to take the picture, and how to control light, focus and exposure. Photographs can document real events, portray people, show landscapes or express an artist's ideas. Unlike the other print media on this site, a photograph is made by recording a scene directly and not by drawing or carving an image.

A brief history

The first surviving photograph was made by the French inventor Nicéphore Niépce in the 1820s. In 1839, Louis Daguerre announced the daguerreotype, a process that produced sharp, detailed images on polished metal plates. William Henry Fox Talbot in Britain developed a method that used a negative to make many paper prints, which shaped how photography worked for the next century. In 1888, George Eastman's Kodak camera and roll film made photography easy for ordinary people. Digital cameras appeared in the late 1900s, and smartphones now put a camera in most people's pockets. Throughout its history, photography has also been argued over as an art form, and today it is widely shown in museums and galleries.

Quick facts

  • Material: light, plus film or a digital sensor
  • Tools: a camera, a lens and often a flash or tripod
  • Captures a scene directly instead of drawing it
  • Invented in the 1800s and now mostly digital
