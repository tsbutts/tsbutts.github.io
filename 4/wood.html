Wood Carving

What it is

Wood carving is the art of shaping wood by cutting it away with tools such as knives, chisels and gouges. Carvers work with the grain of the wood and choose the type of wood to suit the job, from soft basswood for detailed figures to harder woods like oak or walnut for furniture and large sculptures. Carving can be small and delicate, such as a hand-held figure, or large and architectural, such as a decorated doorway. Because the carver removes material and can't add it back, planning each cut matters.

A brief history

People have carved wood for thousands of years, though wood decays, so fewer early examples survive than stone ones. Ancient Egyptians placed carved wooden figures in tombs, and many cultures developed their own traditions, including the totem poles of Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest, carved masks in West and Central Africa, and the decorated churches and furniture of medieval Europe. Wood carving is still practiced today as both a fine art and a folk craft.

Quick facts

  • Material: wood such as basswood, oak or walnut
  • Tools: knives, chisels, gouges and mallets
  • Subtractive: material is removed, never added
  • Used for figures, masks, furniture and architecture
