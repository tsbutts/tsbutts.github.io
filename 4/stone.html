Stone Sculptures

What it is

Stone sculpture is the art of carving figures and forms out of rock. Sculptors work with materials like marble, limestone and granite, removing material with chisels and hammers and smoothing the surface with abrasives. Because carving takes material away and can't add it back, a mistake can ruin a piece, so sculptors plan each cut carefully.

A brief history

Carved stone figures date back to prehistoric times, and ancient cultures including Egypt, Greece and Rome built traditions around them. Greek sculptors prized marble for lifelike figures, and Renaissance artists in Italy, like Michelangelo, revived that tradition with large statues carved from single blocks. Stone is still used by sculptors today, alongside power tools that speed up the work.

Source: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History.

Quick facts

  • Materials: marble, limestone, granite and alabaster
  • Tools: chisels, mallets, rasps and polishing stones
  • Subtractive: material is removed, never added
  • Durable, lasting for thousands of years

Other sculpting media

  • Bronze Castings
