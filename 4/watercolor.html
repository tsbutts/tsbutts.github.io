Watercolor

What it is

Watercolor is made by mixing pigment with a water-soluble binder, usually gum arabic. Artists thin it with water and paint it onto paper, where it dries into soft, see-through layers. Because the paint is transparent, the white of the paper often serves as the lightest color in a painting.

A brief history

Water-based paints have been used for thousands of years, from ancient Egyptian papyrus to illuminated manuscripts in medieval Europe. Watercolor became a popular medium in its own right in Britain in the 1700s and 1800s, when landscape painters used it to capture scenes outdoors. Its light, portable supplies made it well suited to travel.

Quick facts

  • Surface: paper, usually thick and absorbent
  • Transparent, so layers build up gradually
  • Water-based, so it cleans up with water
  • Hard to correct, so artists plan ahead
