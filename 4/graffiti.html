Graffiti

What it is

Graffiti is art made on public surfaces such as walls, bridges and trains, usually with spray paint or markers. It ranges from quick signatures called tags to large, colorful pieces that take hours to complete. Whether it counts as vandalism or art often depends on whether the artist had permission, which is why many cities now set aside legal walls and commission murals.

A brief history

People have scratched and painted marks on walls since ancient times, and writing has been found on the ruins of Pompeii and ancient Rome. The modern graffiti movement took off in cities like Philadelphia and New York in the late 1960s and 1970s, when young writers began tagging neighborhoods and subway cars. Over time the style grew more elaborate, and today it overlaps with street art and large-scale murals shown in galleries and festivals.
