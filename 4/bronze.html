Bronze Castings

What it is

Bronze casting is a way of making sculpture by pouring molten metal into a mold. Bronze is an alloy, mostly copper mixed with tin, that melts at a high temperature and hardens into a strong, durable form. In the lost-wax method, an artist shapes a model in wax, covers it in a heat-resistant mold, melts the wax out, and pours metal into the empty space. Unlike carving, casting lets an artist make more than one copy from the same model.

A brief history

People began making bronze objects more than 5,000 years ago, and the period when it was the main metal for tools and weapons is called the Bronze Age. Ancient Chinese artists cast ritual vessels in bronze, Greek sculptors made lifelike statues, and the Kingdom of Benin in West Africa produced detailed bronze plaques and heads. Bronze is still widely used today for public monuments and fine art sculpture.

Source: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History.

Quick facts

  • Material: bronze, an alloy of copper and tin
  • Method: often lost-wax casting
  • Can produce multiple copies from one model
  • Very durable, so it's common outdoors
