Weaving

What it is

Weaving is the art of making fabric by interlacing two sets of threads at right angles. The lengthwise threads, called the warp, are held tight on a loom, and the crosswise thread, called the weft, is passed over and under them. Weavers choose fibers like wool, cotton, linen or silk, and they can build patterns and pictures by changing colors and the order of the threads. Weaving can produce anything from plain cloth to detailed tapestries.

A brief history

Weaving is one of the oldest textile arts, with evidence of woven cloth going back thousands of years. Ancient Egyptians wove linen, Andean cultures in South America created complex patterned textiles, and weaving traditions developed in Asia, Africa and Europe as well. In medieval Europe, large woven tapestries decorated castles and churches. The Industrial Revolution introduced mechanical looms that made cloth much faster, but hand weaving continues today as both craft and fine art.

Quick facts

  • Material: fibers such as wool, cotton, linen or silk
  • Tool: a loom, which holds the warp threads tight
  • Built from two thread sets: warp and weft
  • Used for cloth, rugs and tapestries
