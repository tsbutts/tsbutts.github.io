Acrylic Painting

What it is

Acrylic paint is made by mixing pigment with a plastic-based binder called acrylic polymer. It's water-based, so it thins and cleans up with water, but once it dries it becomes water-resistant. Because it dries quickly, artists can layer colors in a short time.

A brief history

Acrylic paints were developed in the early to mid-1900s, as chemists worked on synthetic binders. Water-based versions designed for artists became widely available in the 1950s. The medium was popular with modern artists, who liked its bright colors, fast drying time and low cost compared with oil.

Quick facts

  • Surface: canvas, wood, paper or board
  • Dries quickly, often within minutes to hours
  • Water-based, so it cleans up with soap and water
  • Developed commercially in the mid-1900s
