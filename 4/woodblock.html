Woodblock

What it is

Woodblock printing is a way of making images by carving a design into a block of wood, inking the raised surface, and pressing paper against it. The carver cuts away every part of the design that should stay blank, so only the raised lines and shapes pick up ink. Because one block can be inked again and again, the artist can produce many copies of the same image. For color prints, a separate block is usually carved for each color and printed one after another.

A brief history

Woodblock printing began in East Asia, and early examples from China were used to print text and religious images on paper and cloth. A Chinese Buddhist scroll called the Diamond Sutra, dated to the 800s, is one of the oldest known printed books. In Japan, woodblock prints became a major art form in the Edo period, when artists like Hokusai and Hiroshige made colorful ukiyo-e prints of landscapes, actors and everyday life. Woodblock printing reached Europe in the 1400s and helped spread images and books before and alongside movable type. Artists still use it today.

Quick facts

  • Material: wood blocks, ink and paper
  • Relief method: the raised surface carries the ink
  • One block can print many copies
  • Color prints use a separate block for each color
